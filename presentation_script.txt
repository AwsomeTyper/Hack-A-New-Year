PROJECT AEGIS ANALYTICS — PRESENTATION SCRIPT
================================================
~7 minutes  |  Read on iPad while screen recording on MacBook


[0:00] ——— THE HOOK ———
Show: Hero Section (animated counters)

"In 1972, the federal government made a promise: if you're too poor for college, we'll cover the cost. That program — the Pell Grant — once covered 61% of public university tuition.

Today, it covers 25%.

This dashboard is the story of that broken promise — and a data-driven blueprint to fix it."

    → Pause 2 seconds after "25%" — let the counters land.
    → Say: "Every number is live — pulled from the College
       Scorecard API on Data.gov."


[0:45] ——— THE EVIDENCE ———
Show: Evidence Section — scroll slowly through the cards

"We started where every analysis should — by measuring the system as it exists."

PURCHASING POWER EROSION  (point to the Area Chart)

"This chart tracks the ratio of the max Pell Grant to the average cost of attendance over fifty years. The decline is steep and accelerating — coverage has dropped by 36 percentage points."

VIABILITY RISK  (point to the Viability Risk card)

"Next to that, we built an institutional stability forecast. This card scores every institution on a composite index — retention rate weighted 40%, completion rate 30%, Pell dependency 20%, and admission rate 10% — and classifies them into four risk tiers: Critical, Elevated, Moderate, and Stable. It immediately surfaces which schools are most financially fragile."

"Below that is a Grant Elasticity estimate — for every $1,000 increase in Pell funding, our regression model predicts how many additional students would complete degrees."

    → Hover over a tooltip to show:
      "Every metric has a tooltip explaining the calculation
       and data source — complete transparency."

VERTICAL EQUITY  (scroll to the Bar Chart + KPIs)

"We also asked: does the money even reach the neediest students? Across 1,810 institutions, we calculated the average unmet need — the gap between what a family can pay, what Pell covers, and what the school charges. The green reference line on the chart is the max Pell award — every red bar above it represents a school where students must bridge that gap with loans or work."


[2:30] ——— EQUITY MATRIX ———
Show: Equity vs. Excellence scatter plot

"This is where descriptive analysis meets predictive modeling. This is our Equity vs. Excellence Matrix."

"Each dot is one institution. The X-axis measures the Pell completion gap — how much worse Pell students do versus their peers. The Y-axis measures 'Bending the Curve' — a predictive model that calculates whether a school's actual completion rate beats or falls short of what an OLS regression predicts for similar schools."

"Top-right are Equity Champions — they close the gap AND outperform expectations. Bottom-left is the opposite."

    → Click on a dot to show school detail card.
    → Switch the X-axis to a different metric
       (e.g. Instructional $/Student or Median Earnings).
    → Filter by a state or college type.
    → Drag the Pell Rate or Student Size sliders.

"We built custom metrics — not just raw rates, but risk-adjusted performance. The 'Bending the Curve' model on the Y-axis is our core predictive tool — it's an OLS regression controlling for Pell rate, student size, instructional spend, and Carnegie class, with an R-squared of 0.506."

"And the correlation between the two axes is near zero, meaning equity and excellence are independent dimensions — schools don't have to sacrifice one for the other."


[3:45] ——— OPTIMIZATION STUDIO ———
Show: Scroll to Optimizer Section

"With the evidence and predictions clear, the next question is: what should we actually do? So we built an Optimization Studio."

"This uses Linear Programming to answer: given a fixed budget, how should we distribute Pell dollars to maximize total graduates?"

"Three strategies, side by side:"

"Base — distribute evenly by enrollment. Performance — weight toward high value-add schools. Retention Trigger — concentrate on schools where students are at highest dropout risk."

    → Drag the budget slider from $10M to $100M.

"Watch — the predicted impact table updates in real time. Pell students reached, expected graduates, enrollment impact, cost per graduate."

    → Click into the allocation table and scroll.

"You can see exactly which institutions get funded and by how much. Baseline graduates, projected graduates, and enrollment uplift — institution by institution."

"See this badge — Fairness Audited. We run every optimization through the fairlearn library to check for demographic parity. This ensures we're not 'creaming' — systematically excluding schools that serve the neediest populations."

    → Say: "A policymaker could use this today to model the
       impact of a budget increase before it's approved."


[5:15] ——— POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS ———
Show: Impact Section

"The data leads to three evidence-based recommendations."

"One — Index to Actual Costs. Tie the Pell maximum to public tuition rates, not just inflation."

"Two — Risk-Sharing. If a school takes federal aid and fails to graduate those students, require partial repayment."

"Three — Flexible Distribution. Front-load aid and create emergency micro-grants for financial shocks that cause preventable dropout."

"But — any performance-based reform creates pressure to 'cream' — to reject high-risk students to protect metrics. Our fairness audits catch exactly that."


[6:15] ——— CLOSE ———
Show: Scroll back to Hero Section

"Project Aegis Analytics transforms the College Scorecard from a static dataset into a living decision-support tool."

"Descriptive analysis measured the erosion. Our Bending the Curve model and Viability Risk scores predict who's at risk. The Optimization Studio shows exactly where to invest."

"Every metric has a tooltip. Every model is bias-audited. Every recommendation is grounded in real data from Data.gov."

"Thank you."

QUICK Q&A PREP
================================================
• "Bending the Curve" — OLS regression residual as value-add
  (R² = 0.506). Controls: Pell rate, size, spend, Carnegie class
• Fairlearn — demographic parity + equalized odds tests
• Data — College Scorecard API (Data.gov), 1,810 institutions
• Viability composite — retention (40%), completion (30%),
  Pell rate (20%), admission rate (10%)
• Grant Elasticity — regression-based enrollment estimate
  per $1,000 grant increase
• Optimization — PuLP linear programming, 3 strategies
