PROJECT AEGIS ANALYTICS — TWO-PERSON PRESENTATION SCRIPT
==========================================================
~7 minutes  |  Caleb (C) & Jefferson (J)
Demo on MacBook  |  Read from iPad


[0:00] ——— THE HOOK ———
Show: Hero Section (animated counters)

C: "In 1972, the federal government made a promise: if you're too poor for college, we'll cover the cost. That program — the Pell Grant — once covered 61% of public university tuition."

J: "Today, it covers 25%."

C: "This dashboard is the story of that broken promise — and a data-driven blueprint to fix it."

    → Pause 2 seconds — let the animated counters land.

J: "Every number you see is live — pulled from the College Scorecard API on Data.gov. And if you see an info icon, you can hover for the exact source and calculation. Over 30 tooltips — complete transparency."


[0:45] ——— THE EVIDENCE ———
Show: Scroll to Evidence Section

C: "We started where every good analysis should — by measuring the system as it exists."

PURCHASING POWER EROSION  (C points to the Area Chart)

C: "This chart tracks the ratio of the max Pell Grant to the average cost of attendance over fifty years. Coverage has dropped by 36 percentage points — and the decline is accelerating."

VIABILITY RISK  (J takes over — points to the Viability Risk card)

J: "To go beyond just describing the problem, we built a predictive model — this Institutional Viability Forecast. It scores every institution on a composite index — retention rate weighted at 40%, completion at 30%, Pell dependency 20%, and admission rate at 10% — then classifies them into four tiers: Critical, Elevated, Moderate, and Stable."

    → Hover over a tooltip to show the calculation.

J: "Below that, our Grant Elasticity estimate shows that for every $1,000 increase in Pell funding, we can predict how many additional students would enroll system-wide."

MODEL PERFORMANCE  (J points to the Model Performance card)

J: "And this card proves it works. Our Dropout Risk Classifier achieves a 0.795 AUC — that's 5-fold cross-validated on real data. The Bending the Curve model explains about 35% of the variance in completion rates. These aren't hypothetical — the stats update live from the trained models."

STATE RISK DISTRIBUTION  (C scrolls to the State Risk chart)

C: "We also aggregated risk at the state level. This bar chart shows the 15 highest-risk states — you can hover to see how many at-risk institutions are in each one. It immediately surfaces geographic hotspots for intervention."

VERTICAL EQUITY  (C continues — points to the Bar Chart)

C: "Then we asked: does the money even reach the neediest students? Across over 1,800 institutions, we calculated unmet need. The green line is the max Pell award — every red bar above it represents a school where students must bridge that gap with loans or work."


[2:45] ——— EQUITY MATRIX ———
Show: Scroll to Equity vs. Excellence scatter plot
On screen: transition text appears — "which institutions are beating the odds?"

J: "The data above reveals the systemic problem. So now the question becomes: which institutions are actually beating the odds?"

J: "This is our Equity vs. Excellence Matrix. Each dot is one institution. The X-axis measures the Pell completion gap — how much worse Pell students do versus their peers. The Y-axis is 'Bending the Curve' — a predictive model that calculates whether a school's actual completion rate beats or falls short of what an OLS regression predicts for schools with similar demographics."

C: "Top-right are Equity Champions — they close the gap AND outperform expectations. Bottom-left is the opposite."

    → C clicks a dot to show school detail.
    → C switches the X-axis to "Instructional $/Student."
    → C drags the Pell Rate slider.

C: "The model on the Y-axis controls for Pell rate, student size, instructional spend, and Carnegie classification. And the correlation between the two axes is near zero — meaning equity and excellence are independent. Schools don't have to sacrifice one for the other."


[4:00] ——— OPTIMIZATION STUDIO ———
Show: Scroll to Optimizer Section

J: "Understanding the problem isn't enough — decision-makers need to know where to invest. So we built an Optimization Studio."

J: "This uses Linear Programming — the PuLP library — to answer: given a fixed budget, how should we allocate dollars to maximize total graduates?"

C: "Three strategies side by side. Base — distribute evenly by enrollment. Performance — weight toward high value-add schools. Retention Trigger — concentrate on schools where students are at highest dropout risk."

    → C drags the budget slider from $10M up to $50M.

J: "Watch the Key Insight box update in real time."

    → J reads the executive summary sentence aloud, e.g.:
    "At a $50M budget, the Retention Trigger strategy reaches 12,000 Pell students and produces 3,200 additional graduates at about $15,000 per graduate."

    → C scrolls to the allocation table.

C: "You can see exactly which institutions get funded and by how much — baseline graduates, projected graduates, and enrollment uplift, school by school."

J: "See this badge — Fairness Audited. We run every optimization through the fairlearn library to check for demographic parity. This prevents 'creaming' — systematically excluding schools that serve the neediest populations."

    → J says: "A policymaker could use this today to model
       a budget increase before it's even approved."


[5:30] ——— POLICY RECOMMENDATIONS ———
Show: Impact Section

C: "The data leads to three evidence-based recommendations."

C: "One — Index to Actual Costs. Tie the Pell maximum to real public tuition rates, not just inflation."

J: "Two — Risk-Sharing. If a school takes federal aid and fails to graduate those students, require partial repayment."

C: "Three — Flexible Distribution. Front-load aid and create emergency micro-grants for financial shocks that cause preventable dropout."

J: "But any performance-based reform creates pressure to 'cream.' That's why our fairness audits are built in — they catch that before it happens."


[6:15] ——— CLOSE ———
Show: Scroll back to Hero Section

J: "Project Aegis transforms the College Scorecard from a static dataset into a living decision-support tool."

C: "Descriptive analysis measured the erosion. Predictive models identify who's at risk. And the Optimization Studio shows exactly where to invest."

J: "Every metric has a tooltip. Every model is bias-audited. Every recommendation is grounded in real data from Data.gov."

Both: "Thank you."
