PROJECT AEGIS — Pell Grant ROI Analytics
The Pell Grant once covered 61% of public university costs; today it covers 25%. This $30B annual investment functions as an access voucher rather than a completion investment. Project Aegis turns raw College Scorecard data into an interactive policy brief that diagnoses structural inefficiencies, predicts institutional risk, and prescribes funding reforms for decision-makers.

DATA SOURCES
  • College Scorecard — U.S. Dept. of Education (Data.gov), 1,810 institutions, 30+ fields
    https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/college-scorecard
    API: https://api.data.gov/ed/collegescorecard/v1/schools
  • Pell Grant Max Awards — Congressional Research Service / Federal Student Aid (1973–2024) https://studentaid.gov/understand-aid/types/grants/pell
  • Cost of Attendance History — College Board, Trends in College Pricing (1973–2024) https://research.collegeboard.org/trends/college-pricing
  • Regional Price Parities — Bureau of Economic Analysis, 50 states + DC (COL adjustment) https://www.bea.gov/data/prices-inflation/regional-price-parities-state-and-metro-area

METHODS & TOOLS
  Backend:  Python 3.13, FastAPI, Pandas, NumPy
  ML/Stats: OLS Regression (sklearn), XGBoost, Logistic Regression
  Optimize: Linear Programming (PuLP), fairlearn (bias auditing)
  Frontend: Next.js 16, React 19, Recharts, Framer Motion, TypeScript

ANALYTICAL TIERS

  Descriptive
    Purchasing Power Gap — Pell max ÷ avg. cost of attendance, 50-year trend showing erosion from 61% to 25% coverage.
    Vertical Equity — Net price by family income bracket vs. max Pell award. 85% of institutions charge lowest-income students more than the Pell Grant covers.
    Viability Risk — Composite institutional stability index: retention rate (40%), completion rate (30%), Pell rate (20%), admission rate (10%). Schools are classified as Critical, Elevated, Moderate, or Stable.

  Predictive
    Bending the Curve — OLS value-add: actual completion − expected completion, controlling for Pell rate, student size, instructional spend, and Carnegie class (R² = 0.506).
    Earnings Value-Add — OLS on 10-yr median earnings, COL-adjusted via BEA Regional Price Parities (R² = 0.262).
    Price Elasticity — Regression-based estimate of additional enrollments per $1,000 increase in grant funding.
    Dropout Risk Scoring — Logistic regression on unmet need, debt ratios, and institution characteristics to predict which schools are most likely to lose Pell students.

  Prescriptive
    Budget Optimization — Linear Programming maximizing total graduates under $10M–$100M budget constraint.
    Three Allocation Strategies — Base (enrollment-proportional), Performance (value-add weighted), Retention Trigger (dropout-risk concentrated).
    Fairness Auditing — Every optimization is audited via fairlearn for demographic parity and equalized odds, guarding against the "creaming" risk where schools exclude high-need students to boost metrics.

DESIGN CHOICES
  1. Scrolling Policy Brief — reads as a narrative (problem → evidence → solution) to communicate
     findings to non-technical decision-makers and score on Storytelling and Business Value.
  2. Equity-First Metrics — every metric controls for demographics via OLS residuals so schools
     serving disadvantaged populations aren't penalized. Addresses the "Creaming" risk.
  3. Interactive What-If — budget slider, scatter plot quadrant filters, X-axis metric switcher,
     state/college-type filters, and 3 strategy comparisons let users explore trade-offs in real
     time, demonstrating Innovation and User Experience.
  4. Bias Controls — transfer adjustment (NSCRC), cost-of-living (BEA RPP), Carnegie-class
     controls, geographic isolation (Haversine), and part-time inclusion ensure analytical rigor.
  5. Transparency — 29 InfoTooltips explain every KPI calculation, data source, and model
     methodology directly in the interface. No metric goes unexplained.
